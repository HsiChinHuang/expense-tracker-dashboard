## Goal

Turn the milestone's configuration into a MEASURED operational event: run the
t80 `--simulate-fallback` harness against the local backend, keep the real
degraded output, have an AI responder answer over that evidence in the t81
schema, verify recovery with the t82 runbook, and write the three artifacts
into the t83 incident directory. This card is the difference between "we have
alerting files" and "we ran the loop and here is what came out".

- Six dependencies, and each is a real input rather than a formality: t80

## AC draft (groom will fence these - target 5 fenced ACs, inside max_acs=6)

- **ac1 - evidence.json is measured, not authored.** The committed
  `evidence.json` is t80's manifest plus the four captured legs, and its
  content is consistent: the health leg shows `status: "degraded"` with
  `fallback_active: true`, the metrics leg carries the three t79 series and the
  three t78 resource attributes, the log tail names the fallback engagement,
  and the version leg is a commit that exists in this repo's history. The fence
  cross-checks those internal consistencies - a hand-written file with an
  impossible combination fails.
- **ac2 - response.json validates against the t81 schema under the pinned
  validator.** The same command t81 pins runs against the committed
  `response.json` and exits 0; the negative control (a stripped copy) exits
  non-zero. The recorded actions in the response are asserted to be inside
  t82's allow list.
- **ac3 - resolution.md carries the recovery verification and a timeline.**
  It contains the actual `verify-recovery.sh` output showing `status ok` and
  `fallback_active false`, a timeline whose timestamps are monotonic and whose
  events match the evidence timestamps, and the recovery step's measured
  before/after - no assertion of recovery without the output that proves it.
- **ac4 - the chain closes end to end.** The incident ID in `evidence.json`,
  `response.json`, `resolution.md` and the t83 `alert.json` are the same
  constant; the alert name inside `alert.json` is the name t85 will quote; and
  the metrics evidence names match the t76 inventory by extraction. This is
  the leg that makes the reconstruction in t88 a pointer chain rather than a
  narrative.
- **ac5 - delta purity and the read-only boundary held.** Only the three
  allow-listed files are added, all inside the t83 incident directory;
  `alert.json`, both incident READMEs, `incident-response/collect-evidence.sh`,
  `observability/`, `backend/`, `ops/`, `security/`, `openapi.yaml`, `README.md`
  and every `docs/` file are byte-clean against the named immutable ref; the
  bundle contains no secret (grep for `JWT_SECRET`, `.env` content, token
  shapes).

## Constraints (hard)

- Exactly 3 files, all inside
  `incident-response/incidents/2026-10-17-db-fallback-01/`:
  `evidence.json`, `response.json`, `resolution.md`. The raw capture directory
  `evidence/` belongs to t80's harness; if the run leaves raw files behind, the
  card states where they went and why they are or are not committed - it does
  not silently widen the allow-list.
- **Attested-by-reference is a FAIL for this card.** Every claim in
  `resolution.md` must carry the measured output that supports it. A sentence
  like "the collector captured the degraded state" without the captured payload
  is exactly the failure mode this milestone removes.
- The drill runs against the LOCAL uvicorn on the pinned endpoint. No Docker, no
  compose stack, no production or remote host, no port 8001, and no killing of
  any process the drill did not start.
- The responder pass obeys t82's policy: read-only reads plus report writing.
  It may not restart, mutate a database, or merge, and `response.json` must not
  claim otherwise.
- No secret may enter the bundle; the card shows the secret grep.
- No product-code change. If the drill reveals a product defect, this card
  records it in `resolution.md` and the fix becomes a NEW card - the builder may
  not opportunistically patch the backend here.
- Timestamps are real UTC from the run; no back-dating, no invented timeline.
